The winner's curse, in one sentence¶

When you pick the best of many noisy scores, the winning score is biased upwards: the winner was chosen partly for its luck, and luck does not repeat on new data.

The name comes from auctions (Capen, Clapp and Campbell, 1971, bids for offshore oil leases): each company estimates the value of the field with some error, and the one that wins is the one that overestimated it most — so the winner tends to overpay. Our configurations "bid" with their validation scores, and the highest bid is the most optimistic one.

Another picture: give a 10-question true/false quiz to 100 students who all answer at random. The best one scores about 9/10. Give that student a new quiz: about 5/10.

Skill and luck ★¶

Real models are not all equal. Give the 20 models true accuracies evenly spread from 0.50 to 0.60 (np.linspace(0.50, 0.60, 20)); on 50 records, pick the winner, then score it on 50 fresh records. Over 10 000 repetitions: mean selection score of the winner, mean fresh score, mean true accuracy of the winner, and how often the winner is really the best model (0.60).

C5. Within the noise, take the simplest¶

Lecture 10a, Reading the cross-validation results: among the configurations whose mean is within one standard deviation of the best, take the simplest. For a tree, simpler means: smaller max_depth first; for the same depth, larger min_samples_leaf (more customers per leaf, fewer leaves).

This is a heuristic, not a statistical test. The standard deviation of the 5 fold scores is not a confidence interval: the rounds share most of their training data, so they are not independent, and 5 values give only a rough estimate. We use it as a scale for the noise when comparing configurations scored on the same folds — the same scale as the error bars of C4. (Hastie, Tibshirani and Friedman's one-standard-error rule uses std/√k instead, about 2.2 times narrower for k = 5: our version is more conservative.)

  1. Compute the threshold: mean of the best configuration minus its standard deviation.
  2. Select the candidates of res at or above the threshold. How many are there?
  3. Sort the candidates by max_depth (increasing), then min_samples_leaf (decreasing), then mean_test_score (decreasing), and take the first one: call its hyperparameters chosen (a dictionary, with integer values for the two numbers).

D3. Leakage inside cross-validation ★¶

Lecture 10a, Leakage inside cross-validation: a preparation step that computes something from the data must be recomputed inside each round, on the training folds only. The effect can be dramatic. An extreme case, with pure noise:

  • 100 individuals, 5000 random features, a random binary target: nothing can be predicted, the true accuracy is 50%.
  • Preparation step: keep the 10 features whose mean differs most between the two classes.

The cell below creates the data and the selection function (run it, do not change it).

rng_noise = np.random.default_rng(1)
Xz = pd.DataFrame(rng_noise.normal(size=(100, 5000)))
yz = pd.Series(np.repeat([0, 1], 50))

def top_features(X, y, k=10):
    # the k columns with the largest difference between the class means, computed on (X, y)
    diff = (X[y == 1].mean() - X[y == 0].mean()).abs()
    return diff.nlargest(k).index

cv_z = StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=0)
